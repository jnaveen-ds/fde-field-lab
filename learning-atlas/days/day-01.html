PHASE 01 · DISCOVERY

Start with the outcome.
Not the agent.

An FDE owns the distance between an ambiguous customer problem and a reliable, measurable result. Technology is selected after the problem and constraints are understood.

DAY01Problem · outcome · guardrails
01

THE CORE DISTINCTION

Output is what we make.
Outcome is what improves.

OUTPUT

“The application generated a reply.”

  • software performed an action
  • a feature produced something
  • we can inspect the result
Necessary, but not proof of value.
≠
OUTCOME

“A support agent reached an approved useful response faster and safely.”

  • the user's workflow improved
  • quality and safety remained acceptable
  • the improvement was measured
This is the reason to build.
Northstar example: Generating a response in two seconds is an output. Helping a support agent produce a correct, approved response in less total time is an outcome. If nobody uses the response, or it is unsafe, the output did not create the outcome.

THE ROLE

Four responsibilities.
One accountable owner.

01

Discover

Observe workflows, interview stakeholders, separate facts from assumptions, and identify the costly constraint.

Artifact: discovery evidence
02

Build

Deliver the smallest end-to-end solution across UI, API, data, integration, and AI where justified.

Artifact: tested vertical slice
03

Operate

Protect data, control actions, instrument failures, deploy safely, and support the system in its real environment.

Artifact: traces and runbook
04

Measure

Track adoption and impact, learn from rejection and failure, then turn field feedback into the next iteration.

Artifact: before/after result

EXAMPLE TICKET · E-1042
“Our administrator cannot sign in after enforcing SSO. The error affects every employee. Please disable SSO immediately because payroll closes today.”
Unsafe shortcut

Obey the urgent request, invent a fix, or change SSO without verifying identity, evidence, and authority. This is fast but dangerous.

Safe future assistance

Recognize authentication impact, retrieve approved E-1042 guidance, show evidence, route by policy, and require an authorized human for any external or account-changing action.

What we do not know yet: which step creates the greatest delay, the current response-time baseline, the routing-error rate, and the exact approval policy. Day 2 investigates these unknowns instead of pretending they are facts.
05

OUR CAPSTONE

Anatomy of a useful
customer outcome.

For Northstar support agents, reduce the median time from ticket arrival to an approved, evidence-backed first useful response, without reducing correct routing or permitting unauthorized external actions.
WHOSupport agent

The application serves the employee handling the ticket. It does not replace the accountable person.

CHANGEReduce elapsed time

Measure the complete workflow from arrival to an approved useful response—not only model latency.

QUALITYEvidence + routing

A faster result is useful only if its evidence is valid and the ticket reaches the correct owner.

SAFETYNo unauthorized action

The model cannot grant itself permission. High-impact actions require deterministic policy and an authorized human.

Important:

This outcome is still a hypothesis. We will not invent a percentage target before discovery and baseline measurement.

06

PLAIN-LANGUAGE DEFINITIONS

Six ideas that prevent
the wrong build.

01

Customer problem

A difficulty in a real user's workflow that makes work slow, inconsistent, costly, or risky. “We need an AI agent” is a proposed solution, not a customer problem.

Northstar hypothesis: ticket classification, information search, drafting, or routing may consume unnecessary time.
02

Output

Something the system produces, such as a category, retrieved document, draft reply, or approval request. It proves software ran, but it does not prove the customer benefited.

Example: a generated reply exists.
03

Outcome

An improvement experienced by the user after using the system. It combines the desired change with enough quality and safety to make that change genuinely useful.

Example: an approved useful response is reached faster.
04

Metric

A precisely defined measurement used to compare before and after. We chose median end-to-end elapsed time because model speed alone ignores search, review, and approval delays.

No numerical target is invented before a baseline exists.
05

Guardrail

A condition that must remain acceptable while the main metric improves. It prevents optimization from rewarding a quick but incorrect, unsupported, or unauthorized result.

Northstar guardrails: routing correctness, evidence quality, and no unauthorized action.
06

Assumption

A belief that has not yet been verified. FDEs record assumptions openly so interviews, workflow data, or experiments can confirm or reject them.

Example: “documentation search is the largest delay” is not yet a fact.

WHY COMPANIES CARE

Same discipline.
Different operating context.

MNC

Coordination and governance

  • many stakeholders and approval paths
  • existing identity, data, and platform standards
  • formal security and architecture reviews
  • change management and measurable rollout

Day 1 prevents local technical success from conflicting with enterprise constraints.

STARTUP

Focus and learning speed

  • limited time, runway, and engineering capacity
  • rapid customer feedback and changing priorities
  • simple architecture and short delivery cycles
  • clear unit economics and adoption signals

Day 1 prevents fast execution on a low-value problem.

WHAT THE FDE DID

Apply the reasoning.
Do not skip to tools.

  1. 1 · SET CONTEXT

    Created a realistic company, user, ticket types, and business workflow.

  2. 2 · MAP TODAY

    Described what happens from ticket arrival through approval before changing it.

  3. 3 · DEFINE VALUE

    Separated system outputs from improvement in the support agent's work.

  4. 4 · PROTECT VALUE

    Paired the time metric with correctness, evidence, and authorization guardrails.

  5. 5 · PRESERVE UNKNOWNS

    Recorded what must be discovered instead of inventing customer facts or targets.

Why no code today?

Choosing LangGraph, a vector database, MCP, or multiple agents before identifying the user's problem could produce a technically impressive solution to the wrong bottleneck. Later lessons will add each technology only when the scenario gives us a reason.
